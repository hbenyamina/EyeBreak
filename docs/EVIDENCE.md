# EyeBreak: what the evidence says

A literature review of EyeBreak 3.1's health behaviour, October 2026: eye breaks, blinking, interruptions, movement,
feedback and water. Each feature gets the evidence, a verdict (Keep / Change / Remove / Add) and the change to make.

**Method.** Three independent reviews (eyes; breaks, interruptions and habits; water) searched PubMed, PMC, the ACM
Digital Library, Cochrane and position statements (AOA, AAO, TFOS, EFSA, IOM, WHO, ACSM, NIOSH, ESPEN). The studies
that would change the app's defaults were then checked against their PubMed abstracts or full text:
Johnson & Rosenfield 2023, Talens-Estarelles 2023, Redondo 2025, Wolffsohn 2025, Xu 2025, Desai 2026 (PUSH) and the
Yamada 2022 equation (PMC full text).

**Evidence quality.** **Strong**: several controlled studies or meta-analyses agree. **Moderate**: one good
meta-analysis or a few controlled trials. **Weak**: small, uncontrolled or indirect. **None**: nothing found.

Nothing here is medical advice; it's what the app should and shouldn't claim.

## Verdicts at a glance

## 1. Eye breaks

**The 20-20-20 rule has little direct support.**
- Johnson & Rosenfield 2023 (*Optom Vis Sci*, crossover, n=30, 40 min reading task): 20 s breaks every 5, 10 or 20 min
  made no difference to symptoms (P=.70), reading speed or accuracy, compared with no break. **Weak.**
- Talens-Estarelles 2023 (*Cont Lens Anterior Eye*, single arm, n=29, 2 weeks of reminders): symptoms fell (CVS-Q 11→8,
  OSDI 24.6→19.0), signs didn't (tear break-up, staining, redness); benefits faded a week after stopping. **Weak.**
- Redondo 2025 (*Exp Eye Res*, crossover, n=24): breaks every 10 min or self-paced reduced burning and eye strain and
  the short-lived blur after near work. **Weak–Moderate.**
- TFOS Lifestyle report (Wolffsohn 2023, *Ocul Surf*): support for the rule is "limited". Reviews (Sheppard &
  Wolffsohn 2018; Coles-Brennan 2019; Kaur 2022) recommend frequent breaks on the strength of occupational trials.

**Longer, hourly breaks have the better record.**
- Galinsky 2000 (*Ergonomics*, n=42) and 2007 (*Am J Ind Med*, n=51): four extra 5 min breaks a day (about one an hour)
  lowered eye strain and discomfort with no loss of output. **Moderate.**
- Balci & Aghazadeh 2003 (n=10): 30 min on, 5 min off gave the least eye strain. **Weak.**
- Albulescu 2022 (*PLoS ONE* meta-analysis, N=2,335): breaks of ≤10 min raised vigour (d=0.36) and lowered fatigue
  (d=0.35); longer breaks predicted better performance. **Moderate.**

**Distance and length.** Focusing demand is 0.17 D at 6 m, 0.33 D at 3 m and 1.7 D at 60 cm, so anything past ~3 m
is effectively as good as 20 ft (optics, **Strong**). The blur left after near work takes 30 s to ~2 min to clear
(Lin 2012), so 20 s is at the short end.

**Blinking is the active ingredient the app under-uses.**
- Screen work cuts blinking from ~15–18 to ~4–9 a minute (Patel 1991; Tsubota & Nakamori 1993; Freudenthaler 2003),
  and raises incomplete blinks (Argilés 2015). Incomplete blinks track symptoms better than blink rate (Portello 2013).
- Kim 2021 (*Cont Lens Anterior Eye*, n=41): a 10 s blink exercise every 20 min for 4 weeks cut OSDI 36→22 and
  incomplete blinks 54%→34%. Wolffsohn 2025 (*CLAE*, RCT): adding a squeeze step helped; the best routine was 15
  close–squeeze–open repetitions 3× a day. Xu 2025 (*npj Digit Med*, RCT, n=40): a prompt every 5 s for two squeeze
  blinks cut OSDI 31→19. **Moderate** (all in people with dry eye).
- One "Blink" pill every 10 min has no direct evidence: tested cues run every 5–8 s or are structured exercises.

**Eye exercises.** TFOS 2023 calls the evidence "insufficient or unconvincing" outside convergence problems. Following a
dot keeps the eyes focused at screen distance, which is the opposite of resting them. **Weak/None.**

**What the app should do.** Every break: "look out of a window or across the room" for 20 s, then two close (2 s),
squeeze (2 s), open cycles; about 30 s in all, with the instruction in place of the quote. About every hour, a 5 min
break. Optional dry-eye mode: 15 squeeze blinks, 3× a day.

**Other advice worth one-time tips:** screen 50–70 cm away, top at or below eye level (AOA; Rempel 2007);
brightness matched to the room; text ≥12 pt; a desk humidifier (Wang 2017, RCT); see an optometrist for persistent
strain (uncorrected astigmatism matters). **Don't claim**: blue-light filters help (Cochrane, Singh 2023: probably
not); outdoor time helps adult eye strain (its evidence is for myopia in children).

## 2. Interruptions

- **Timing.** The same interruption costs 3–27% more time, about twice the errors and up to twice the annoyance
  mid-task than at a task boundary (Bailey & Konstan 2006; Adamczyk & Bailey 2004). Deferring notifications to a
  detected breakpoint (mean 89 s) cut frustration (Iqbal & Bailey 2008, *CHI*). **Strong.**
- **Volume.** Even 2.8 s interruptions double sequence errors (Altmann 2014). Batching notifications 3× a day improved
  attention and mood (Fitz 2019, RCT n=237). Acceptance of reminders fell 30% for each extra one in the same encounter
  (Ancker 2017). **Moderate.**
- **Compliance.** People take about half of software breaks (SuperBreak, Morris 2008: 50–66%; Time for Break, Luo 2018:
  40% yes). The top reasons for no were "busy", "in a meeting" and "just had a break". Break software improved
  perceived recovery in a cluster RCT (van den Heuvel 2003, n=268). **Moderate.**
- **Forced vs. voluntary.** System-timed breaks beat self-timed ones (Henning 1989; McLean 2001; Biwer 2023), but no
  trial compares full-screen blocking with a dismissible prompt, and autonomy over breaks predicted their benefit
  (Trougakos 2014). People mostly use snooze for short same-day delays (Weber 2018).

**EyeBreak today** sends about 13 prompts an hour: 3 breaks, 3 warning toasts, ~1.3 water toasts and 6 nudges.
**Change:** ≤3 an hour; wait for a typing pause (up to 2 min); stay quiet with the camera or microphone on, while
screen sharing or presenting; carry the blink and the water prompt inside the break; turn the warning into a menu-bar
cue; add "5 more minutes" (at most twice, not a skip); and don't schedule a break right after a natural one.

## 3. Movement

Breaking up sitting with light walking lowers post-meal glucose and blood pressure and improves mood: 2 min every
20 min (Dunstan 2012, *Diabetes Care*, n=19); 5 min every 30 min (Duran 2023, *MSSE*, n=11). Prompts to stand cut long
sitting bouts (Cochrane, Shrestha 2018, low quality; Evans 2012). **Moderate** for short-term effects. **Add:** make
the hourly break "stand up, refill your water, look out of a window": posture, water and far focus in one interruption.

## 4. Feedback and goals

- Monitoring progress raises goal attainment (Harkin 2016, *Psychol Bull*, k=138, d=0.40). **Strong.**
- Missing one day doesn't materially affect habit formation (Lally 2010, n=96). Seeing a broken streak lowers later
  engagement, less so when the streak can be repaired (Silverman & Barasch 2023, *J Consumer Res*). A "welcome back"
  bonus after a miss was the best of 54 gym interventions (Milkman 2021, *Nature*). **Moderate.**
- Feedback about the task helps; feedback about the person can hurt (Kluger & DeNisi 1996). Range goals ("2–4") keep
  people engaged longer than a single number (Scott & Nowlis 2013). **Moderate–Strong.**

**Keep** the rhythm line: a miss looks recoverable, which is what the evidence favours. **Change** the streak: it
counts calendar days, so on a Sunday–Thursday week it resets every weekend; count work days with grace instead.
Derive the daily goal from the shift (a fixed 15 is ~62% of an 8 h day at 20 min, ~94% under Pomodoro). Keep insights
factual ("Most skips came between 2 and 4 pm"), not judgemental.

**The dry-eye image.** Fear appeals help a little (Tannenbaum 2015, d=0.29) and only with a clear action; threat
without efficacy produces avoidance (Witte & Allen 2000; Peters 2013). Nothing shows two skipped breaks cause bloodshot
eyes. **Remove**; replace with: "Screens cut blinking by about two-thirds. Two slow, full blinks now?" [Do it] [Later].

## 5. Water

**The calculator.** "30–35 ml/kg" is a clinical rule for *total* water (food and drink); NICE uses 25–30 ml/kg for IV
maintenance. EFSA's 2.0/2.5 L, IOM's 2.7/3.7 L and WHO's figures all include food water (20–30%); ESPEN 2022 converts
EFSA to drinks at 1.6 L (women) and 2.0 L (men). Water turnover rises only ~14 ml per kg of body weight (Yamada 2022,
*Science*, n=5,604, eq. 1: `1076·PAL + 14.34·kg + 374.9·male + 5.823·RH% + 1070·athlete + 104.6·HDI + 0.4726·alt −
0.3529·age² + 24.78·age + 1.865·T² − 19.66·T − 713.1`). So 33 ml/kg runs ~25% high for most people and much higher for
heavy people (120 kg → 3.96 L vs ~2.6 L).

**Recommended drinks goal:** `base + 10 × (kg − ref) + exercise + heat`, base 1,600 ml / ref 60 kg (women),
2,000 / 70 (men), 1,800 / 65 (not stated); exercise 200 ml (moderate) or 400 ml (vigorous) per 30 min of deliberate
exercise (ACSM: tailor to losses; ~1–1.5 ml/kcal); +500 ml only in heat without air conditioning (Yamada's temperature
term; NIOSH guidance for physical work in heat). Round to 50 ml, keep the calculator within 1,200–4,000 ml.

**Safety.** Kidneys clear about 0.5–0.9 L an hour (Noakes 2001; Perez-Gomez 2022); hyponatraemia comes from drinking
beyond losses (Hew-Butler 2015). A 10 min interval with 250 ml glasses prompts 1.5 L/h. Keep prompted drinking under
~500 ml an hour (minimum interval = glass ÷ 500 ml × 60 min), glasses ≤500 ml, and warn above 4 L a day.

**Does it matter at a desk?** Mild dehydration (≥1.4% body mass) worsens mood and fatigue (Armstrong 2012; Ganio 2011)
and, above 2%, attention (Wittbrodt 2018 meta-analysis, d=−0.28). Below 2% the cognitive effect is uncertain. Thirst
works for most healthy adults (IOM). There's no evidence that drinking more helps dry eye (TFOS 2023; Nguyen 2023), so
water stays out of the eye messaging.

**Do reminders work?** Modestly: a smart bottle with reminders added ~0.6 L of urine a day in stone formers (Stout
2022); the 1,658-person PUSH trial (Desai 2026, *Lancet*) raised urine volume a little and didn't reduce stones. Logging
progress itself helps (Harkin 2016).

**Logging.** Count tea, coffee and other non-alcoholic drinks one-for-one (Killer 2014; Maughan 2016; IOM; WHO). Reset
the reminder whenever any drink is logged. Judge "behind" from the running total
(`expected = shift target × elapsed ÷ shift`), and dip the line only when a glass behind; Esc keeps meaning "not
drinking now".
